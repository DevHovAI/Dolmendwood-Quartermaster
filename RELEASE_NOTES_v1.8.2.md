Shops become places you travel to, learn to buy things back, and finally say what is in your pockets.

## A shop can be a particular shop

A shop was a catalogue with a price on it. It can now be the cheesemonger, the pipe carver, or the magicians' guild.

- **Services** — an entry that grants no item is used where it is bought and never enters an inventory. **Twenty-one specialists from the Player's Book** come as a library: an attended bath, a tattoo, engraving, identification, a guide, dedicated research. You can add your own, reprice any of them, and now also prune the library again — removing one of the book's own specialists is not a deletion but a restoration of the printed price.
- **A shop may keep to its own shelf** and show nothing from the catalogue at all. An empty category list has always meant "sells everything", so until now there was no way to say "sells only cheese".
- **Stock that may not be there.** Give a line an X-in-6 chance and it is on the shelf some visits and not others. Rolled from the shop's own visit counter rather than the dice, because shelf contents are scenery and six dice in chat per shop is not.

## Selling

Set a shop's **buy-back rate** on its map note or market stall and the party can sell to it. Nothing appears until you do: most shops in the settlement notes only sell.

- The rate is reckoned on **an item's own value**, not on that shop's asking price — a shop that charges double does not therefore pay double.
- **A shop buys only what it deals in.** A cheesemonger has no use for your sword and no way to price one.
- **A half-empty quiver is worth half a quiver.** A quiver of 20 costs 5gp and one arrow 25cp, so the price of a quiver *is* its arrows — a quiver with seven left fetches seven arrows. Bundles sell by the loose torch, containers by how full they are, everything else by the piece.
- Anything carrying a container of its own is left out: selling a backpack would have to decide what happens to everything inside it.

## The map means something again

**A player opens a shop, market, inn or hoard only where the party is standing** — the same hex on a hex map, or simply the same scene where there is no hex grid, so a village is a village once you are in it. The Referee is never restricted, and one setting turns the whole rule off.

Name your party's marker under **The party's marker on the map** if you travel as a single token that belongs to no player; leave it empty and every character's own token counts.

The **general shop** — the one belonging to no map note, reachable from anywhere at any time — is now off for players by default. It was the door that made the map notes optional.

## What is in your pockets

The shop **says what the buyer can spend**. It has always known — the Affordable filter is built on exactly that figure — but never printed it, so the one number you want while shopping was the one you had to leave the shop to find. The Affordable filter now also reaches the shop's own shelf, where it never used to.

## Smaller things

- A **Shop button on the scene toolbar**, and the toolbar and day bar agree on one order: inventory, trash, shop, inn, loot.
- The day bar's backpack opened the **whole party's** inventory for players. It now opens their own, as the toolbar always did.
- The thirteen treasure categories are gone from the shop forms. A shop sets treasure aside *before* it applies the category list, so ticking Potions showed nothing at all.
- The disclosure arrow in **From Catalogue** never drew — it asked for FontAwesome 5 and 6 and Foundry ships 7. Smaller tickboxes, too.
- Windows that grow to fit now stop at the edge of the screen instead of walking off the bottom with the Create button.
- Players' **Add Custom Item** can be switched off, for a table where everything should come from the catalogue or the Referee's hand.
- A failed action used to look exactly like a dead button. Everything that can refuse now says why — including telling a player that no Referee is connected to carry it out.
